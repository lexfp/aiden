Evil’s Eye

	Prologue

The sun hung low in the sky, casting long shadows through the tiny windows of the humble house. The air was thick with the scent of earth and warm banana leaves, but despite the quiet, a soft yet persistent voice pierced the stillness.
"Get up, dear! You can’t sleep through this one!" It was his mother again, her voice laced with excitement. "You have to come with me on the magma tour of Three Volcanoes! It’s a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity!"
But all he wanted was peace. Peace and quiet.
"I’m fine here, mom," he muttered, pulling his blanket over his head, hoping the familiar fabric would somehow drown out her calls. "I’ll just eat my rawten bananas and relax today."
He could already taste the sweet, slightly tangy fruit on his tongue, their golden skins soft yet firm, just ripe enough to enjoy. Rawten bananas, his favorite food, were the perfect snack. They didn’t need to be cooked or prepared; just peel, bite, and savor.
But there was no escaping the relentless pressure of his mother’s enthusiasm. She had always been like this—full of energy, eager to show him new things, to push him out into the world.
"No, no, no! You’re coming. It’s important for you to understand the history of this place. You have to see it with your own eyes!" Her voice was impossible to ignore now, each word sharp, like a small whirlwind pulling him in.
Reluctantly, he rolled out of bed. His feet hit the cool stone floor with a soft thud, the coldness of the room seeping into his bones. He felt like a piece of wood in the water, floating aimlessly with no real direction. The magma tour? Honestly, he had no interest. But there was no point in arguing with his mother when she was this determined.
So, he dragged himself into his boots, threw on his cloak, and followed her out the door. He couldn’t help but feel the weight of his disappointment settle over him like a thick fog. It wasn’t that he hated the idea of the volcanoes, but if he was being honest, he didn’t care much for the tours themselves. His heart wasn’t in it. His heart was elsewhere, dreaming of what he could become.
The magma tour wasn’t just a typical sightseeing trip. No, it was a ritual for those who were to be tested in their elemental potential. Three volcanoes—each representing a powerful force of nature—stood as silent guardians of the ancient lands. And while most people came to admire the molten flows and the roaring eruptions, his mind couldn’t help but wander to a different kind of journey: the journey of self-discovery.
You see, each volcano represented a different elemental affinity, a path that one could potentially take if they proved themselves worthy. The Volcano of Air, towering in the distance, was known for its windswept peaks, where the greatest Air elementals practiced their craft. Then there was the Volcano of Fire, where flames licked at the sky, testing those brave enough to control the inferno. And last, the Volcano of Earth, solid and unwavering, a symbol of stability and strength.
But today, it was the magma tour—focused on the Volcano of Fire—and the opportunity to understand what it might be like to wield the destructive power of flame.
As he trudged along, the heat from the volcano in the distance grew stronger, the very air vibrating with the promise of power. But for him, it was never about fire.
His mind often wandered to the Traitor Z Tour, which focused on the village’s founding legend—the mysterious founder who, after a series of betrayals, had joined forces with the Kitsune. The Kitsune, a race of magical foxes, had granted the founder an egg, a magical egg that would hatch into hundreds of baby kitsune, each one possessing the ability to control an element. Some were masters of air, others of fire, earth, and water. But there was one who could control them all: the All-Elemental.
The All-Elemental. The one person who could wield all the elements at once.
What a thought. The sheer power, the freedom. He fantasized about what it might be like—walking through life, untouchable, capable of bending the very forces of nature to his will. No more restrictions. No more fear of being caught using his abilities in school, where elemental powers were strictly banned after the infamous Death Toll Incident years ago.
The school had been a place of tragedy, a place where the use of elemental powers had gone unchecked and led to an unthinkable disaster. The deaths had been so high that the very idea of elemental magic was now taboo within the walls of the school. The thought of it made him shudder.
That’s why he had to keep his powers hidden, even from his closest friends. Even now, when he was deep in thought, he was careful not to accidentally shift the air around him. The risk of getting caught was too great. He had learned to suppress his abilities over the years, like a dam holding back an unstoppable river.
“You’re daydreaming again, aren’t you?” came a voice, breaking through his thoughts.
He looked up to find a familiar face: Fenn, his old friend. The one with the Water Element. Fenn was easygoing, always the one to offer a playful grin when things got too heavy. They had grown up together, and Fenn’s knack for controlling water was something that had always fascinated him.
“You know you don’t have to do this tour, right? You could just come with me and let’s have some fun instead.”
Fenn’s eyes glinted with mischief. He had never been the one to stick to the rules, and honestly, that was something he admired.
“You could come with me and just—”
“I can’t,” he interrupted, half-smiling despite himself. “I have to see this through. You know how mom is. She’s got this whole… plan for me.”
The two of them stood there for a moment, the world around them quiet except for the distant rumble of the volcano.
“Yeah, I know. But we both know you’re not gonna find what you’re looking for here.”
Fenn had a point. This wasn’t the place for him to discover his true calling. But what if it was? What if, by some twist of fate, he did end up finding the right element for himself today? What if this magma tour could somehow show him the way?
For a moment, doubt and hope warred in his chest.
Finally, Fenn grinned, slinging an arm around his shoulder. “Alright, buddy. But don’t come crying to me when you realize you’re too hot to handle.”
He couldn’t help but laugh at the pun. It felt good, for just a moment, to forget about the heavy weight of his ambitions.
“Bubble blast!” Fenn the Water Elemental mewed with a mischievous grin on his face, his voice bubbling with glee. He raised his hand, and with a flick of his fingers, a huge sphere of water shot out from the tips of his fingers, dousing his friend completely. The cold, cascading wave splashed across his chest, soaking him through in an instant.
“Ha ha! Hee hee hee! Har har!” Fenn cackled, his laugh echoing like ripples on the surface of a pond, filled with so much energy that even the air around them seemed to hum. His laughter was contagious, and soon, they were both laughing—uncontrollable, wild laughter—spinning in circles, their voices bouncing off the rocky cliffs nearby.
“Boo!” Fenn shouted, jumping out from behind a boulder. The sound of his voice, combined with the sudden splash of water, was like an explosion of joy. His friend’s soaked form staggered, nearly tripping over his own feet as the laughter continued.
But as the laughter echoed into the distant, fiery skies, something strange happened.
The ground beneath them began to shift. The soil cracked, groaned, and then... disappeared.
A strange, unsettling silence followed. Then, from the edge of the void, a purple pee-colored monster emerged. It moved with a strange grace, its feet never touching the ground, walking across the chasm of nothingness. Its long limbs twisted unnaturally, and its eyes glowed with a cold, unfeeling light. The monster didn’t fall into the void. No, it walked on it. As if the void itself was its domain, something it had mastered.
For a brief, fleeting moment, his mind caught the possibility—What if I can do that too? What if I could walk on the void like that purple monster? Without thinking, he rushed forward, pushing his legs into the deep, yawning emptiness.
At first, it felt fine. He felt like he could stand, just like the monster had.
But then—CRACK!
His legs sank deep into the void, and he felt himself tumbling forward, spiraling into nothingness. His heart raced, panic setting in. The world around him dissolved into the black abyss, the sound of his breath cutting through the silence.
Suddenly, everything stopped.
He woke up—somehow. His mind was foggy, his body sore from the fall, but there was something even more pressing.
He was surrounded.
Tiny, ferocious creatures—hungry squirrels—circled him like a pack of wolves, their beady eyes locked onto him with an unsettling intensity. Their fur was slick, almost metallic in the dim light. They chattered wildly, their sharp teeth clicking as they seemed to move closer.
For a moment, he froze. He remembered his friend Fenn’s warning about the squirrels. He’d heard tales of these creatures—squirrels that had developed a strange hunger for magic, for power, and for anything that was not firmly planted on the ground.
With his powers locked away and his fire abilities still on hold, he knew he was in trouble. The squirrels wouldn’t care if he was an elemental. They only cared about one thing: their next meal.
He would have to wait—wait until his fire powers were recharged. But that wasn’t something he could rush. He didn’t want to create a void fire, a disastrous phenomenon that could destroy everything in its path, including himself. If that happened, the consequences would be severe.
He had heard whispers in the elemental world: those who created void fires were sent to the Three Hundred Waters, where they would be tried and sentenced to death. Even Fenn, his friend, a water elemental who understood his struggles, knew how dangerous it could be to meddle with the void.
It wasn’t just about control. It was about survival.
And so, he waited.
He waited, silently, surrounded by the squirrels, feeling the weight of his powerlessness press down on him.
The hours dragged on.
He couldn’t use his fire power yet. Not until the air around him was calm and his body was ready to unleash it. But every second felt like an eternity. He kept his movements slow and deliberate, trying to avoid the squirrels' sharp gaze. They were hungry, but they weren't foolish. They could sense fear.
Finally, after what seemed like an agonizingly long day, his body felt the familiar warmth of his elemental power stirring within him. The fire, once dormant, began to flicker back to life. He could feel it in his chest, in the tips of his fingers. He clenched his fists and took a deep breath, ready to face what came next.
But then...
“Bubble blast!”
Once again, Fenn’s voice rang out, playful and mischievous as ever. The water elemental had arrived, just in time to splash him with another burst of water.
“Hey!” Fenn grinned, his eyes twinkling with the thrill of it all. “You look like you could use some refreshing. Don’t worry, I’m here to help!”
“Fenn, seriously, I—” He started, but Fenn was already laughing, throwing another orb of water that drenched him from head to toe.
At that moment, all he could do was laugh, too. The squirrels scattered at the new wave of water, their attention momentarily distracted. The ground beneath him felt a little more solid, and the fire in his chest burned a little brighter.
“Alright, alright! I’ll get you back for that, don’t worry!”
But deep down, he knew he had learned something important today. The void wasn’t just a place to fall. It was a place where the rules of the elements bent and twisted, a place where even the strongest could lose their footing. But it was also a place to grow. And someday, maybe, he would learn to walk across it with ease, like that purple monster.
But not today.
Today, he would just enjoy his laughter, the company of his friend, and the fire that was waiting to blaze.
As he left the void behind, exhaustion and relief mingled within him—finally, he was free. The eerie emptiness of the void was replaced by the familiar, comforting surroundings of his world. Yet, his reprieve didn’t last long. Standing in his path was someone he knew too well: the Water Wielder. His oldest friend. But this wasn’t a happy reunion. The intense glare in his friend's eyes sent a chill down his spine. Something was wrong.
“Why does he keep showing up here of all places?” he muttered, unease growing by the second.
Before he could utter another word, his friend raised his hands and cast a spell. "Bubble Warp!" The cry rang out, and within moments, a shimmering bubble wrapped itself tightly around him. Water filled the bubble, choking him and stealing the air from his lungs. Desperate to survive, he clawed at the encasing, but it was futile. Panic surged through him as his vision blurred.
But he wasn’t ready to give up. Summoning every ounce of strength, he let out a defiant yell, "Choked Fire Grasp!" Fire erupted around him, encasing him in a brilliant inferno. His hands began to change, morphing into fierce, dragon-like talons that gleamed with primal power. The bubble couldn’t withstand the heat—it shattered, releasing him from its suffocating grip.
He staggered forward, his talons still burning with energy. He barely had time to marvel at the transformation; his focus was on his friend. Determined to mend whatever had gone wrong, he rushed to his companion, hoping this was all a misunderstanding. But dread froze him in his tracks. His friend lay motionless on the ground. Lifeless.
“No...” His voice broke as he dropped to his knees beside his fallen friend. Tears streamed down his face as he whispered, "Why did it have to come to this? Why?"
Frantic, he rummaged through his inventory, hoping for some kind of remedy or revival spell. His hands trembled as he searched, but all he found was a peculiar item—a shaded blue apple. Its faint, otherworldly glow caught his eye. He hadn’t realized how hungry he was until he held it in his hand. Hesitant, yet desperate for strength, he took a bite.
As he chewed, a rush of energy coursed through him—a sensation at once familiar and foreign. This was the same feeling he’d experienced when he first gained the element of fire. But now, it felt different, deeper, as though another layer of power had awakened within him. It was undeniable—he’d been gifted with another elemental force.
Standing tall, his fiery talons ablaze, he resolved to uncover the truth behind his friend's death, the events in the void, and the mystery of the elements that seemed to shape his destiny. His journey was far from over—it had only just begun.
…the only strange thing was the fire left behind had a peculiar hue—not the usual vibrant orange and red, but a swirling mix of blue and gold. It pulsed as if alive, refusing to entirely die out despite the water elementals' combined efforts. This fire wasn’t ordinary; it carried an energy that resonated with something ancient, something primal. The leader of the water elementals squinted at the lingering embers, his jaw tightening.
“This isn’t normal fire,” he muttered to himself. “It reeks of both creation and destruction… Could it be that he’s begun to tap into the forbidden elements?”
The others whispered amongst themselves, but the leader silenced them with a raised hand. “We’ll not debate his destiny here. Prepare the scouting parties. He couldn’t have gone far. And send word to the Elder Seer in the Land of Shadows—we need confirmation.”
Meanwhile, he stumbled through the dense forest on the outskirts of the town, his heart pounding in his chest. The guilt was unbearable; his anger had burned away, leaving a hollow ache in its place. He had killed his friend, taken his element, and now he was hunted. As he stopped to catch his breath, the weight of it all pressed down on him, nearly driving him to his knees.
But there was no time for sorrow. His thoughts were interrupted by the sensation of something stirring within him. He checked his abilities once more, and this time, it wasn’t just fire and water listed there. A new symbol had appeared, faint and unfamiliar, shimmering with a strange glow. He reached out to it with his mind, and as he did, a vision overwhelmed him—a dark, foreboding land, with shadows crawling across the ground like living creatures. In the vision, a voice echoed through his mind, soft but unrelenting.
“Come to me.”
The words left him shaken, but also curious. Who was calling him? And what was this power awakening within him? He didn’t understand it yet, but one thing was clear: he couldn’t go back. Not to the streets he’d scorched, not to the life he’d known. If he wanted answers, he’d have to follow the pull of the vision, even if it meant stepping into the unknown.
As he made his way toward the horizon, the forest seemed to grow darker, the air heavier with each step. The world around him felt alive, almost watching, as though it, too, knew of his destiny

	Part 1
“SLAM!” The sound reverberated through the house. Sholo barely flinched—he was used to it by now. With five siblings constantly wreaking havoc, it had become the soundtrack to his life. Whether it was broken dishes, slammed doors, or toppled furniture, destruction seemed inevitable in his chaotic household.
But amidst all the noise, one thing grated on Sholo’s nerves more than anything: his oldest brother, Shama. Shama had unusually large ears that seemed to wiggle when he smirked, a small nose that made him look perpetually irritated, and a round figure that earned him the nickname "fat monkey" among their classmates. Shama had perfected the art of making Sholo’s life miserable, poking fun at him every chance he got.
Today, however, something was different. Sholo wasn’t feeling the usual fear that kept him quiet and compliant. He wasn’t shrinking into a corner, trying to avoid Shama’s attention. Today, a spark of defiance burned in his chest. He was fed up. He wouldn’t let Shama push him around anymore.
“Mommy, Sholo is getting all angry!” Shama whined in the most exaggerated tone possible, dragging out the words as if they were meant to sting.
Sholo’s fists clenched, but he didn’t respond. He had a secret, one he hadn’t dared to share—not with Shama, not with his siblings, not even with his parents. Deep down, he knew that he had the highest chance of achieving something extraordinary. Something none of them could imagine. Something that would prove Shama’s taunts wrong. He had the highest chance of getting into the academy for elements.
This was amazing because there, he would prosper with the element of dragons. Unlike traditional elements such as fire, water, earth, or air, the element of dragons defied classification. It technically wasn’t an element in the conventional sense; it was far beyond such limitations. The element of dragons was a fusion—a harmony between power, wisdom, and instinct. It wasn’t just about raw force; it carried the essence of ancient knowledge, a connection to the primal energies that shaped the world long before humanity’s understanding of elements began.
Harnessing the element of dragons wasn’t easy—it required not just strength but a deep sense of purpose, an unwavering resolve to align oneself with forces far greater than personal ambition. It was said to grant its wielder unmatched abilities: the power to summon storms and calm them with a single word, to weave through dimensions with unparalleled agility, and even to glimpse into the threads of fate itself. But this power came at a cost. Those who bore the element of dragons were marked, bound to an eternal quest that tested the very depths of their soul.
The thought filled Sholo with hope and trepidation. If he could truly master the element of dragons, he would no longer be the scared, insignificant boy his brother taunted every day. He would become something greater—a force that no one, not even Shama, could look down upon. But he knew the path ahead wouldn’t be simple. To prosper, he would have to prove himself worthy, not only to the dragons but to the whispers of power that echoed in the farthest reaches of existence.
Sholo couldn't help but feel a mix of awe and determination as he thought about the element of dragons. If he could master it, everything would change. No longer would he be the boy afraid to stand up to his brother. But he knew the path to such power wouldn’t be easy. He’d have to leave his home, face trials, and prove himself worthy—not just to the dragons, but to his own fears.
As Sholo stepped outside, the evening air greeted him with a soothing breeze. His siblings' usual chaos raged behind him, but he felt strangely detached, almost as if the noise was fading. It was time to embark on his journey. He couldn’t wait any longer.
Gathering his few belongings, Sholo slipped through the streets of his town unnoticed, heading toward the outskirts where, according to whispers he'd overheard, the first step to unlocking the dragon's element awaited. It was said that a cave deep in the forest held the Dragon’s Gateway—a mystical portal that allowed only those with the heart of a dragon to enter.
As night descended, the forest loomed large in front of him. Shadows stretched across the ground, and the rustling leaves created an eerie symphony. Sholo’s breath quickened, but he pressed on, guided by an unshakable resolve. The deeper he ventured, the darker the forest became. Strange noises echoed around him, and for a moment, he thought he saw movement in the corners of his vision. Was he being followed? He didn’t know, but he tightened his grip on his belongings and quickened his pace.
Finally, after what felt like hours, he reached a clearing. At the center stood the cave—the Dragon’s Gateway. The entrance shimmered faintly, as if it were alive. Sholo’s heart raced. This was it. The beginning of his destiny. But just as he stepped forward, a voice stopped him in his tracks.
“You think you’re ready for this?”
Sholo spun around, his eyes darting through the darkness. A figure emerged from the shadows—a tall, imposing man with piercing eyes and an air of authority. He carried a staff adorned with symbols of fire and earth.
“Who are you?” Sholo asked, his voice trembling.
“I am Erydan,” the man said, his tone calm but firm. “Guardian of the Gateway. You can’t pass until you prove yourself.”
Sholo took a deep breath. He had been expecting a challenge, but the sheer presence of the Guardian was intimidating. “How do I prove myself?”
Erydan smirked, raising his staff. “The element of dragons isn’t just about power. It’s about balance, wisdom, and courage. To pass, you must face three trials—one for each of these virtues. Fail even one, and the Gateway will reject you.”
Sholo felt a lump in his throat but nodded. “I’m ready.”
Erydan stepped aside, and the cave entrance began to glow brighter. “Enter, if you dare. The first trial awaits.”
Sholo swallowed hard, clutching his belongings tightly as he stepped into the cave. The air inside was thick, almost oppressive, and the glowing walls seemed to pulse with energy. He didn’t know what awaited him, but he knew he couldn’t turn back now.
When Sholo first stepped through the gates of the Elemental Academy, his heart pounded with a mix of excitement and trepidation. The sprawling campus before him was unlike anything he had ever seen. Towering spires reached into the clouds, each representing one of the elements taught within the academy—fire, water, earth, air, light, and darkness. Streams of energy flowed through the air, crackling with power, and students moved in clusters, their conversations punctuated by bursts of magic as they demonstrated their abilities.
Sholo adjusted the strap of his bag, his fingers brushing against the few belongings he had brought. The weight of the dragon element within him, still new and unfamiliar, made him feel both exhilarated and uncertain. He reminded himself why he was here. This was the place where legends were made, where the most talented elemental wielders honed their powers. And he was determined to carve out his place among them.
As he walked through the grand courtyard, he felt the gazes of other students. They whispered amongst themselves, sizing him up. Sholo wasn’t oblivious to their scrutiny, but he kept his head high. His goal wasn’t to impress them—not yet. He was here to grow stronger, to prove that he deserved to stand among the best.
His first day was a whirlwind. Professors introduced the curriculum, explaining how each element had its own tower, each with specialized training grounds and mentors. There were mandatory lessons on elemental theory and history, but the real excitement lay in the practical sessions—duels, challenges, and live exercises that tested students’ abilities in real-world scenarios.
But it didn’t take long for Sholo to realize one harsh truth: here at the academy, his mastery of the dragon element didn’t make him special. It made him an anomaly. Most students specialized in one element, dedicating their lives to perfecting it. The dragon element, however, was rare and misunderstood, straddling the boundaries of fire, air, and something entirely other. Sholo’s professors struggled to fit him into the standard curriculum, and his performance reflected the disconnect. On paper, his grades were average—nothing that hinted at the potential burning within him.
He refused to let this discourage him. During quiet moments in the evenings, when the campus was still and the towers bathed in moonlight, Sholo trained alone. He experimented with his dragon element, pushing its limits and discovering new applications. He combined the elegance of air with the ferocity of fire, creating techniques that left even him in awe. These were the moments that reminded him why he had come here.
It wasn’t long before Sholo’s hunger for more power and his desire to prove himself drove him to a bold decision. If he couldn’t rely on the academy to recognize his potential, he’d show them through action. His plan was simple: challenge other students to duels and claim their elements as his own. He didn’t fully understand why he was able to absorb their powers when he won—it was an ability tied to the mysterious nature of the dragon element—but he wasn’t about to waste it.
His first challenge was a water wielder named Torrin, known for his precision and strategic mind. The duel was intense, with Torrin’s water techniques threatening to overwhelm Sholo’s fiery nature. But through grit and ingenuity, Sholo found a way to turn Torrin’s own element against him, claiming victory and absorbing the essence of water into his own being.
Word of Sholo’s success spread rapidly. Some students admired his audacity; others grew wary of him. But Sholo wasn’t concerned with their opinions. He had a mission, and he would see it through.
