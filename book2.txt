The Tournament of Maka
Prologue
Gogatsu stepped out into the sunlight as he and his brother finished eating. The new fighting tournament is up, he thought. I wonder if the legendary fighter Aisha2 will be there.
As he left for the academy, a person in dark robes approached him. The man muttered something Gogatsu couldn’t make out, and when Gogatsu tried to walk past, the man stopped him.
“Wait,” he ordered.
The man introduced himself as Katatusu, but Gogatsu noticed something odd—his name tag read “CFU.”
“Why is the name on your name tag CFU?” Gogatsu asked suspiciously.
Katatusu just laughed and shoved him aside. “See you around,” he said with a grin.
Gogatsu looked more closely at Katatusu and realized his face wasn’t a mask. It was human—except for a high-tech device covering his eyes and nose. When Katatusu turned around, Gogatsu saw that his back was robotic.
“Wait a second. Come back here!” Gogatsu called out.
Katatusu didn’t respond. He kept walking. Gogatsu broke into a run, but Katatusu turned and punched him in the face, then followed with an uppercut. He looked like he was about to teleport but instead left all his belongings behind in a messy heap.
Gogatsu returned home, but his brother was gone. After searching the entire mansion, he found a note. It wasn’t from his brother—it was signed by CFU.
I wonder who CFU is, he thought.
He read the note:
Come to find the academy. There you will find nothing. If you find anyone, just know they are me. Look around for a holographic device; it is actually a—
—From CFU
That was all it said.
Why are so many strange things happening to me? Gogatsu wondered. The cyborg guy… CFU… that’s the name on that cyborg’s tag!
Now the only mystery was what the rest of the name meant. Then it hit him—he remembered the device on the cyborg’s back. He rushed out the door, returned to the heap of belongings, and dug through it until he found the device.
It read: Cyborg Fighter Ultimate Going To Tournament.
CFU… Cyborg Fighter Ultimate, Gogatsu realized.
He didn’t need any more clues. He hurried to the academy and found the cyborg—now wearing different clothes—waiting at the entrance. The cyborg told Gogatsu to seek out the Shunonakamaitsu, the legendary sword that would allow him to win the tournament.

Chapter 1:   Arena
Aisha2 jumped out of bed and rushed to the warp opener, ready to teleport to the arena. The morning air was crisp, and the crowds were already cheering. This was the first tournament to include fighters from all realms.
A man from the demon realm approached him. Without greeting Aisha2, he simply said, “Seek the Shunonakamaitsu—the legendary sword that holds the spirit of one of your ancestors.”
Aisha2 didn’t hesitate. He opened his warp opener and teleported to the place the man had called the Garden of Sabo.
There, he met a man who looked unusually tall for someone in their eighties. The man handed Aisha2 a katana without saying a word—then suddenly attacked him.
But Aisha2 was ready. He leapt into action, slicing the air with his new weapon. The man removed his human disguise, revealing a glowing cross on his chest. It was actually a case for an enchanted dagger.
The soul-human whipped out the dagger and stabbed the ground. Then he handed Aisha2 a note, grinning with a flashy, evil-eyed smile.
The note read:
It’s been a while, Aisha2. It’s me—Sabkushi.
I came to tell you to beware. The federation might not be able to stand up to the armed forces.
The armed forces have discovered how to control the essence of life.
The time is coming soon.
You will either give up all hope for the tournament or find me—and fight me to the death.
I also must tell you something important.
The emerald of elements is currently being held by not me but—
The message was cut off.
Whoever this soul essence monkey was, he had information Aisha2 needed. But who could have the emerald?
Then it hit him like a boulder—Kamakichi, the legendary soul king. He was the father of the strongest man in the world.
Aisha2 wasn’t afraid of him. There was solid proof that Kamakichi couldn’t even do one push-up. He was also known as Maka, a name meaning “weak-stubborn-idiot”—and it wasn’t a lie.
Kamakichi relied on his knights for everything, yet his kingdom still held the most influence over the Overlings World, a realm created solely for powerful kingdoms. His son—the strongest man in the world—ruled a stronger kingdom, but Kamakichi still held sway.
Rumors swirled about the son attempting to kill his father, but there was no solid proof. Not until the reporters started acting like real reporters. The current ones were lazy, unmotivated, and barely covered breaking news or major headlines.






Chapter 2: The Blade of Shadows
Aisha2 stepped deeper into the Garden of Sabo, the wind whispering through the twisted trees like voices from forgotten realms. The katana in his hand pulsed faintly, as if sensing something unnatural nearby.
Suddenly, the air grew colder.
A shadow moved across the ground—too fast, too silent. Aisha2 spun around, blade raised, but saw nothing.
Then, from the mist, a figure emerged.
He wore a cloak darker than midnight, stitched with runes that shimmered like dying stars. His face was hidden beneath a hood, but two glowing crimson eyes pierced through the veil.
“I’ve been expecting you,” the figure said, his voice like gravel grinding against steel.
Aisha2 tightened his grip. “Who are you?”
The figure stepped forward, revealing a weapon unlike any Aisha2 had ever seen. It was a hybrid—half dagger, half sword. The blade was jagged and asymmetrical, forged from obsidian and infused with a violet energy that crackled like lightning. One side was short and curved like a dagger, perfect for close combat. The other extended into a long, serrated edge, designed to cleave through armor and bone alike.
“I am Master Varkul, keeper of the Blade of Shadows,” he said. “And you… you are the one who seeks Shunonakamaitsu.”
Aisha2 didn’t respond. He could feel the power radiating from Varkul’s weapon—it wasn’t just enchanted. It was alive.
“You’re not ready,” Varkul continued. “But I will test you. If you survive, you may earn the right to wield true power.”
Without warning, Varkul lunged.
His movements were impossibly fast. The dagger side of his blade slashed toward Aisha2’s throat, while the sword side came down like a guillotine. Aisha2 barely dodged, rolling to the side and countering with a wide arc of his katana.
Steel met shadow.
The clash sent a shockwave through the garden, uprooting trees and scattering leaves like ash. Aisha2 leapt back, breathing hard. Varkul hadn’t even broken a sweat.
“You fight well,” Varkul said, circling him. “But you rely too much on instinct. You must learn to see through darkness.”
Aisha2 narrowed his eyes. “Then teach me.”
Varkul paused. “Very well. But know this—if you fail, your soul will be bound to my blade for eternity.”
He raised the Blade of Shadows, and the duel began anew.
Aisha2 was ready for him this time with one blow he sacrificed his arm to use his most powerful move.
“One Punch!” Aisha2 attacked and drove his hand into Varkul and finished him off.
By now Aisha2’s hand was turning into darkness and slowly crippling.

Chapter 4: The Tournament of Maka Begins
The warp opener rested in Aisha2’s palm, its surface warm and vibrating faintly as if it could sense what lay ahead. The darkness that had claimed his arm no longer thrashed or spread; instead, it clung to him like a second skin, silent and patient. Sybrian’s words echoed in his mind again and again, not as a threat, but as a promise—when the brackets break. Aisha2 didn’t know what that meant yet, but he understood one thing clearly: whatever the tournament was meant to be, it would not remain that way for long.
Without hesitation, he activated the warp opener.
Reality collapsed inward. Light stretched into thin, blinding lines, and sound vanished as the space around him folded. For a brief, disorienting moment, Aisha2 felt weightless, suspended between realms. Then the ground slammed back beneath his feet.
The Arena of Maka rose around him in impossible scale.
Massive pillars of living stone formed a perfect ring, each one carved with ancient symbols representing realms long thought destroyed. The arena floor shimmered like metal and sand fused together, constantly reshaping itself in preparation for combat. Above, the sky was not a sky at all—it shifted colors and textures, reacting to the presence of fighters as portals tore open across its surface. Floating platforms filled with spectators hovered in the air, stacked layer upon layer, holding beings of every shape and origin. Crystalline screens reflected the scene across dimensions, ensuring that countless worlds bore witness.
The noise was overwhelming.
Cheers, roars, mechanical shrieks, and psychic pulses collided into a single thunderous presence that pressed against Aisha2’s mind. Yet he stood still, calm, eyes forward. He had walked through corrupted gardens, faced living weapons, and stood before a transcendent being who bent reality without effort. A crowd would not shake him.
A voice boomed across the arena, amplified by something far beyond technology.
“WELCOME, WARRIORS OF ALL REALMS!” it announced, the words shaking the pillars themselves. “WELCOME TO THE FIRST MULTI-REALM TOURNAMENT OF MAKA! HERE, POWER WILL BE TESTED. HERE, LEGENDS WILL FALL. AND HERE—ONLY ONE WILL STAND!”
Portals erupted across the arena floor.
Fighters emerged one by one. A demon prince wreathed in hellfire, his wings folded like weapons behind him. A towering mech-knight, gears rotating and energy coursing through exposed conduits. A beast fighter marked with glowing runes, its eyes burning with ancient instinct. Each arrival was met with a surge of cheers from the stands.
Then another portal opened.
Aisha2 felt it before he saw him.
Gogatsu stepped onto the arena floor, his presence quieter than the others but heavier somehow, as if unseen forces were already orbiting him. Aisha2’s gaze locked onto him for only a moment. There was no recognition, no nod—just the understanding that their paths were now converging whether they wanted them to or not.
High above the arena, seated upon a throne of pale crystal, was Kamakichi.
He looked almost disappointed. Thin arms rested lazily on the armrests, his posture slouched, his expression bored. Knights in ornate armor stood rigidly behind him, their weapons gleaming. To the untrained eye, Kamakichi appeared weak, insignificant—just as the rumors claimed. But as Aisha2 glanced toward him, the darkness in his arm pulsed once, sharp and deliberate.
Not weak, it warned.
A bell rang, deep and resonant, cutting through the chaos.
The air shimmered as a massive holographic bracket unfolded above the arena, stretching from one end of the sky to the other. Names flickered into place, glowing in different colors depending on realm and rank. Some sparked with energy. Others glitched briefly before stabilizing.
Aisha2’s name burned crimson.
Match One: Aisha2 vs. Jirushee — Water Realm Champion
The arena floor shifted violently. Stone cracked and sank as water surged upward, forming a vast circular pool. Platforms rose and fell like slow, breathing lungs, while mist rolled across the surface. The scent of salt and pressure filled the air.
From the opposite side, Jirushee stepped forward, water spiraling calmly around his arms. He laughed, the sound carrying easily across the arena.
“I warned you,” Jirushee called out, shaking his head. “This place kills people like you.”
Aisha2 slowly drew his katana. Shadows slid along the blade, curling and recoiling as if alive. His corrupted arm flexed, perfectly obedient.
“Then don’t blink,” Aisha2 replied.
The horn sounded.
Water exploded upward as Jirushee launched forward, the first strike of the Tournament of Maka crashing into motion.
Far beyond the reach of the crowd, Sybrian watched from nowhere at all, his gaze fixed not on the fighters—but on the structure of the tournament itself.
And deep beneath the arena, something ancient shifted in its sleep, responding not to cheers or power…
…but to Aisha2’s presence.
The water surged and twisted, forming a spiraling vortex around Jirushee as he prepared to strike, and Aisha2 tightened his grip on his katana, the shadows along the blade flickering like they had a mind of their own. Every muscle in his body tensed as the corrupted arm pulsed, warning him that the clash would be far from ordinary. The crowd’s roar escalated into a deafening wall of sound, hundreds of thousands of spectators screaming and cheering, yet above it all, Aisha2 felt a subtle, unnatural vibration in the air, a presence that did not belong. His instincts screamed, but before he could react, the world itself seemed to stutter. The water froze mid-vortex, suspended in perfect arcs, droplets hovering like suspended stars. Jirushee’s eyes widened in shock, his attack stalling in midair. The ground beneath the platforms trembled as if the arena itself had sensed an intrusion, a violation of its rules.

A shadowy figure descended from above, landing on the arena floor with a mechanical grace that drew gasps from the audience. Gogatsu would have recognized him immediately if he were present—CFU. The cyborg’s high-tech visor glinted under the arena lights, the back-mounted systems humming with a quiet power that suggested this was no ordinary interference. Every step he took seemed to bend reality slightly, creating faint distortions around his feet. Even Kamakichi’s bored expression flickered for a moment, a tiny crease of curiosity breaking through his mask of disinterest.

The water around him rippled, then disappeared completely, leaving only the wet stone floor glistening under the shifting sky.

CFU’s gaze swept over him directly, the visor reflecting a strange mix of red and blue light, and for the first time, Aisha2 sensed a recognition beyond the professional distance of a stranger. This cyborg was not here as an opponent, nor as a referee. He was here as a guardian, a force that existed outside the normal flow of the tournament. The true test of Maka is only beginning, and there are players in motion you cannot yet see.” Then, with a blur of mechanical speed, he rose into the air, the distortion around him rippling the edges of the arena, and vanished into a sudden warp, leaving behind only the faint hum of his systems and the lingering weight of authority.

Jirushee looked at Aisha2, frustration and relief warring in his expression, while Aisha2 stared at the space where CFU had disappeared, his mind racing with questions. Why had the cyborg intervened? What did he mean by corrupted brackets? And, most importantly, what did CFU know about Sybrian Akaku—and the darkness now permanently clawing at Aisha2’s arm? The tournament had just begun, yet already the threads of power, control, and destiny had been tangled beyond recognition. Somewhere, high above the arena, a shift in the air suggested that the real battle—the one that would determine everything—was only just beginning.
The arena fell silent. Even the crowd seemed to hold its breath, sensing the shift before it fully happened. Jirushee, still standing on the stone platform where the water had receded, narrowed his eyes at CFU’s departure. “You can’t just stop a fight and leave!” he shouted, the words echoing oddly in the warped acoustics of the arena. But Aisha2 didn’t respond; he could feel it—the unnatural weight in the air, a pressure unlike anything he had experienced before. Shadows along his katana shifted nervously, as if whispering warnings. Something was coming. Something final.

The mechanical hum of his systems intensified, filling the arena with a sound that made the hairs on Aisha2’s neck stand on end. He moved toward Jirushee, who raised his hands defensively, summoning ripples of water that flickered into translucent shields. With a single flick of his mechanical arm, a concentrated beam of energy—sharp, pure, and impossibly fast—sliced through Jirushee’s defenses. The beam struck with a force that seemed to bend reality itself, hitting Jirushee directly in the chest. For a fraction of a second, time seemed to stretch; his eyes widened in disbelief, and water magic flared violently, spiraling outward in a desperate, chaotic explosion. Then, in a blink, it ended.

There was no scream, no cry for mercy—only the faintest ripple in the air as the essence of his being dissipated into nothingness, sucked into the residual energy of CFU’s attack. Aisha2 felt a cold chill run through him. His hand tightened on the katana, the shadows along the blade twitching nervously. The darkness inside his arm seemed to shiver in recognition of the absolute finality of the act. Even the arena itself seemed to recoil, the ground beneath the stone platforms cracking slightly, the air vibrating with an invisible tremor.

Every action has consequence. The tournament had changed in an instant. No longer was it a simple test of skill—it was now a lesson in survival under rules that no one could fully understand.
Aisha2 lowered his katana slowly, staring at the spot where Jirushee had been, and felt the shadows along his arm pulse in warning and anticipation. He understood now: nothing in the Tournament of Maka was predictable, and every step forward would demand more than courage—it would demand comprehension, adaptation, and perhaps even sacrifice beyond his imagination. Somewhere above the crowd, the holographic bracket flickered and glitched, as if acknowledging that the game had just changed in ways no one could yet control. And high above, unseen by all but the most attuned, Sybrian’s presence stirred again, a ripple moving through reality itself, watching, waiting, and calculating the moment the true chaos would begin.

Chapter 5: Gogatsu Steps Into the Arena
Gogatsu adjusted the straps of his gear as he stepped through the shimmering portal into the Arena of Maka. The massive pillars of living stone loomed above him, their surfaces etched with the sigils of countless realms, each one pulsing faintly with energy that whispered of the battles that had taken place there long before he was born. The roar of the crowd hit him immediately, a tidal wave of sound that threatened to swallow him whole, yet he pushed forward, keeping his steps measured. Around him, fighters from every corner of reality were assembling: some with armor that gleamed like molten metal, others with weapons that seemed impossibly alive. Gogatsu’s own hands tightened around the hilt of his blade, a weapon simple in design but finely balanced, a reflection of the discipline he had honed over years of relentless training.
The holographic bracket above flickered, reshaping itself as names scrolled into their first matches. Gogatsu’s name glowed gold—an invitation and a warning. His first opponent appeared in a burst of smoke and fire, a towering creature with limbs that ended in razor-sharp talons, a being that had clearly never known defeat. Its eyes glowed with a predatory intelligence as it assessed Gogatsu, and for the first time, he felt the weight of the tournament press down on him. Yet there was no fear. He had faced chaos before, endured punishment, and emerged stronger each time. This fight would be no different, he told himself.
The bell rang.
The creature lunged, moving faster than the eye could follow, a blur of claws and teeth aimed directly at Gogatsu’s chest. He pivoted, sidestepping, his blade cutting through the air to meet a claw that had already swung past him. Sparks erupted as steel met hardened talons, and the shockwave threw Gogatsu back a step. He rolled, springing to his feet, and launched a counterattack, slashing at the creature’s side. The blade struck true, leaving a deep gash, but the creature barely flinched. Its regenerative aura began knitting the wound closed even as it roared, a sound that made the pillars tremble.
Gogatsu forced himself to breathe evenly, letting instinct guide him. He had trained for this exact moment—not merely to fight, but to analyze, to predict, to adapt. Each movement of the creature carried a rhythm, and he began to match it, his attacks flowing like water, striking precisely when the opening appeared. The creature stumbled slightly, surprised by the sudden precision and speed, and Gogatsu pressed the advantage, his blade carving arcs of shadow in the arena air. The crowd erupted, sensing the struggle, their cheers rising and falling like the tide.
But then the creature roared, summoning the raw energy of its realm. The arena floor cracked beneath its feet, jagged stone spikes rising in an instant, aiming to impale Gogatsu as he dodged. He leapt, swinging his blade to deflect one of the spikes midair, feeling the vibration run through his arms. The shadows along the weapon seemed to respond, flickering more violently, hinting at some latent energy within him that even he had not fully tapped. With a well-timed feint and a burst of speed, Gogatsu closed the distance, his blade slicing cleanly across the creature’s chest. This time, the wound did not close.
The creature staggered back, growling, and Gogatsu took a deep breath, raising his blade in preparation for the final strike. He channeled everything he had—his training, his instincts, the silent lessons learned from his brother and the cyborg who had first warned him of the tournament’s stakes. With a single, powerful arc, he drove the blade downward, striking the creature’s core. There was a blinding flash as the energy of the strike met the being’s essence, and then the creature collapsed, leaving nothing but a faint shimmer in the air where it had stood.
The arena erupted into a cacophony of cheers and gasps, the spectators recognizing the skill and decisiveness of Gogatsu’s victory. Yet as he stood there, chest heaving, eyes scanning the crowd and the bracket above, he could feel it—the subtle but unmistakable pull of forces far beyond this fight. The darkness of the tournament, the unseen influence of Sybrian Akaku, and the quiet shadow of CFU’s authority all pressed against him. This was only the beginning. The Tournament of Maka had revealed its first challenge, but Gogatsu understood fully now: the true battles were not just against the fighters he could see, but against the rules, the manipulators, and the unseen forces shaping every outcome.
Gogatsu lowered his blade slowly, scanning the arena. Somewhere high above, a faint ripple of distortion caught his eye, a shimmer that hinted at a presence watching every fight. His mind tightened like a spring. The tournament was far from over, and he had just taken his first step into a game whose stakes were higher than victory or defeat—they were life, death, and the balance of realms themselves.

The platforms trembled, the water from his halted battle against Jirushee rippling faintly like echoes of a past fight, and the holographic bracket overhead flickered, twisting in ways that made it impossible to follow. Shadows along his corrupted arm twitched, warning him of a presence that was far stronger than any single opponent he had faced so far. It wasn’t Jirushee, it wasn’t the audience, and it wasn’t even Kamakichi. It was Sybrian.
The void-like distortion in the arena deepened as Sybrian’s influence became visible—not in body, but in the structure of reality itself. The platforms began folding in on themselves, merging, and vanishing. Fighters who had been positioned for combat were thrown into disarray, blinking out of existence or appearing hundreds of meters away. Aisha2 could feel the pressure of his corrupted arm intensifying, as though the darkness itself recognized the danger. Every instinct screamed that the tournament was no longer a place for conventional combat; it was becoming a trap, a cage that could collapse around him at any moment.

Sybrian’s expression was both solemn and determined. “You survived what no one else did. But surviving alone is not enough,” he said. The heir, still bruised and haunted by the loss of his fallen brother, nodded, his jaw set with determination. “I need to be stronger,” he said quietly, “strong enough to stop this. To protect Vanguard. To protect everyone.” Sybrian’s eyes softened, but there was no hesitation in his voice. “Then I will teach you the God style. Not just a fighting technique, but a philosophy of absolute control over your body, mind, and spirit. Every movement, every strike, every thought must flow as one. Only then will you be able to match the power of beings like Pyroclast.”
The initial sessions were grueling. Sybrian’s training method was unlike anything the heir had experienced. It began with meditation, not just in stillness but in motion, where he had to balance across floating platforms as energy streams twisted unpredictably beneath him. Each step, jump, and landing required perfect control of balance, timing, and energy manipulation. Bladehound and Pluppy assisted, their powers adding resistance and chaos to sharpen focus. Bladehound would suddenly shift from Normal to Fighting Form mid-air, forcing the heir to react instinctively and redirect energy in real time. Pluppy’s bursts of chaotic energy introduced variables impossible to predict, teaching the heir adaptability.
Once the physical foundation was laid, Sybrian introduced the core of the God style: the integration of inner energy, or essence, with external combat. “Every attack begins before it manifests,” Sybrian explained. “Your mind, your body, and the energy within you are already shaping the strike before your opponent even perceives it. Anticipation, control, and precision—they are the pillars of God.” The heir struggled at first, unable to harmonize his energy with motion. Every failed attempt sent ripples through the floating platforms, throwing him off balance, testing his resilience.
Sybrian intensified the regimen. He created simulated combat scenarios within layered rifts, each iteration escalating the speed, power, and unpredictability of opponents. Some were manifestations of Pyroclast’s fire and lightning attacks, others were constructs of elemental chaos designed to overwhelm the senses. The heir was forced to think, react, and strike before the attacks even began. Sweat poured, energy flares erupted uncontrollably, and at times, the heir collapsed in exhaustion—but Sybrian was patient, guiding, correcting, and pushing him beyond limits he did not know he possessed.
Weeks passed, though time felt distorted in the training dimension. The heir’s movements began to synchronize with his internal energy, strikes flowing like currents of light and power, dodges as fluid as water, and defenses as solid as obsidian. He could anticipate energy fluctuations, redirect elemental forces, and even manipulate residual rifts in the environment to his advantage. Each day, Bladehound and the Sus Monkeys participated in the exercises, providing both unpredictability and companionship, forcing the heir to coordinate with multiple allies in chaotic scenarios.
Finally, Sybrian introduced the culmination of God: the divine strike. “This is the essence of the style,” he said. “You are no longer reacting. You are the motion, the energy, and the strike itself. You move before you move, strike before you strike, defend before the attack even begins.” The heir centered himself on a floating platform above a simulated volcanic fissure. His energy flared, glowing in perfect synchronization with his body. Bladehound shifted into Fighting Form and orbited him, Pluppy pulsed with chaotic energy, and the Sus Monkeys moved unpredictably to simulate the chaos of a battlefield.
With a deep breath, the heir executed his first complete God-style strike. Time seemed to bend around him—the motion was preemptive, a perfect blend of anticipation, energy, and execution. A simulated Pyroclast construct that had previously overwhelmed him shattered before the strike even fully manifested, as though the attack had occurred before it was even realized. Sybrian nodded, eyes gleaming with approval. “You are ready,” he said. “Not just ready to fight—but ready to lead, to protect, and to face the impossible.”
The heir, breathing heavily but resolute, looked up at Sybrian. “Thank you,” he whispered. “I will not fail again. I will honor my fallen brother… and I will make Pyroclast pay.” Sybrian placed a hand on his shoulder, the weight of responsibility heavy but shared between mentor and student. “Remember this,” he said. “God is not merely a style—it is a state of being. Master it, and no force, no matter how powerful, will take you by surprise. Fail, and the universe will remind you of its unforgiving nature. Train, fight, and live as God, and you will define the future.”
As the sun—or its equivalent in the pocket dimension—rose over the shifting plains of the training world, the heir stood taller, energy radiating in synchronized harmony with his body. Bladehound and Pluppy moved at his side, the Sus Monkeys chattering in approval, and Sybrian knew that he had forged not just a fighter, but a new vanguard of hope. The next time Pyroclast—or any BEAST operative—emerged, the heir would be ready, and this time, he would not fall.

Chapter 38: Shadows, Fire, and God
The battlefield above the Perilous Rift had grown even more chaotic since Pyroclast’s emergence. Lava jets hissed as molten rivers snaked across fractured plains, lightning tore jagged paths across the sky, and the scorched remains of the previous Apex patrols smoldered in dark smoke. Every shift in terrain seemed alive, resonating with the elemental fury of the villain who dominated it. The air itself crackled with heat and static, making it difficult to even step forward without risking injury.
From the swirling shadows above, a figure descended silently, materializing from a crack in reality itself. Cloaked in shifting darkness, the Shadow Dimension Traveler was a master of stealth, rift manipulation, and kinetic energy absorption, moving as though the shadows themselves obeyed him. His eyes glimmered like obsidian mirrors, and his blades shimmered with a black-and-purple energy that seemed to consume light. He landed lightly on a raised platform of cooled magma, scanning the battlefield, his instincts immediately detecting the immense threat Pyroclast posed.
Without hesitation, the Traveler engaged. With a flick of his wrist, shadow blades extended like tendrils, slashing at Pyroclast’s legs, wrapping around molten limbs, and attempting to siphon energy from the elemental titan. Sparks, fire, and lightning erupted as the shadow blades met molten rock and plasma currents. Pyroclast roared, a wave of fire and lightning erupting from his body, melting the shadows like candle wax. The Traveler leapt into the air, twisting and phasing through rifts to avoid direct contact, each move precise yet desperate.
The battle raged in dizzying speed. Shadow blades coiled around Pyroclast’s limbs, energy siphons activated, and the villain staggered momentarily—but Pyroclast’s raw power was overwhelming. Lava blasts erupted beneath the Traveler’s feet, lightning arcs turned his rift portals unstable, and the sheer heat burned through even the advanced armor he wore. Every strike was countered, every rift manipulated against him. It became clear that Pyroclast was not only strong, but adaptive, learning from every maneuver, anticipating every feint.
Minutes stretched into what felt like hours as the Shadow Dimension Traveler’s movements grew more strained. His energy flares sputtered, and one miscalculation sent him crashing through a molten fissure. He struggled to rise, but Pyroclast loomed above him, flames and lightning converging in a crushing arc. The villain’s roar shook the rift plains, and for the first time, the Traveler realized he might not survive. His shadow energy faltered, rifts destabilized, and the oppressive heat and electric currents bore down on him mercilessly.
It was in that moment that a surge of energy cut across the battlefield. From a stabilized rift above, Vanguard’s surviving brother descended, his body radiating the synchronized energy of the God style. Every movement flowed with precision, anticipation, and preemptive force. Blade and lightning fused into perfect arcs as he landed in a crouch, immediately intercepting the descending arc of fire and electricity that would have crushed the Traveler. Sparks erupted as the clash of energy redirected Pyroclast’s assault, giving the Traveler a moment to recover.
The heir’s presence shifted the tide instantly. God-style anticipation allowed him to predict Pyroclast’s movements before they happened. Each step, each pivot, each strike was a preemptive calculation of the villain’s next move. Shadow blades and God-style strikes coordinated seamlessly as he moved between Pyroclast’s attacks and the Traveler’s maneuvers. Lava streams became platforms, lightning arcs were redirected, and every environmental hazard was transformed into an advantage.
Pyroclast howled in frustration, fire and electricity converging into a storm of annihilation around him. The heir closed the distance with unerring precision, weaving through molten jets, absorbing kinetic backlash into his God-style core, and striking with twin blades that radiated plasma fire and storm lightning. Each strike tore into Pyroclast’s molten armor, exposing fissures in the villain’s body, and leaving glowing cracks that flickered under the intensity of God-style energy.
Finally, the decisive moment arrived. The heir focused, breathing deeply as he merged anticipation, body, and energy into one instantaneous flow. Blade and lightning coalesced into a single divine strike—a slash of energy that existed before it was even released. Pyroclast swung his arms in a desperate, final assault, but it was too late. The God-style strike cut through the elemental titan, severing molten limbs, ripping through the core of his electric-fire energy, and dispersing his energy in a blinding explosion of fire, lightning, and rift energy.
When the light and heat subsided, the battlefield was eerily silent. Pyroclast’s molten form had crumbled into lifeless rock, his energy dissipating into the unstable rifts. The Traveler staggered to his feet, still catching his breath, and looked at the heir with awe. “I… I thought he was unstoppable,” he whispered. The heir, chest heaving but unwavering, extended a hand to help the Traveler fully recover. “No one is unstoppable,” he said quietly. “Not even Pyroclast.”
As the molten plains began to cool, Sybrian watched through a stabilized rift, relief and pride in his eyes. The heir had not only survived the ordeal but had mastered the God style in combat, turning what could have been a catastrophic loss into a decisive victory. Bladehound and Pluppy moved across the battlefield, stabilizing the rifts, while the Sus Monkeys bounded across the plains, checking for lingering energy pockets. The Perilous Rift, once a zone of utter devastation, now seemed calm, almost reverent, as though acknowledging the power of those who had survived.
The Shadow Dimension Traveler finally approached the heir, bowing slightly in respect. “You’ve surpassed what I thought possible,” he said. “Today, you became a force of legend.” The heir nodded, exhaustion and satisfaction mingling in his eyes. “We fight for those who cannot, and we fight to prevent the next Pyroclast from rising. This is only the beginning.”
Sybrian’s voice came through the rift, calm yet commanding. “Prepare yourselves. BEAST will not remain idle. The Reaper now coordinates their operations, and there are more forces coming. But today, you proved that even the strongest threats can fall when skill, strategy, and courage align. Vanguard’s legacy is alive, and it will continue to grow.”
As the sun—or whatever light remained in the distorted rift skies—rose over the cooled lava plains, the battlefield was quiet but charged with promise. The heir, the Shadow Dimension Traveler, Bladehound, Pluppy, and the Sus Monkeys stood together, ready to face whatever new terrors BEAST, the Grim Reaper, or other interdimensional threats would bring. And somewhere in the distance, the rift energy pulsed, signaling that this was far from the last battle.

Chapter 39: Facing the Master of Reality
The battlefield above the Perilous Rift had become a nightmare of shifting landscapes and chaotic energy flows. Lava rivers twisted into frozen plains, jagged rocks dissolved into swirling clouds, and the sky itself split and recombined in impossible geometries. Sybrian Akaku’s heart pounded as he took in the destruction; every second brought new distortions, and every breath felt like inhaling raw, unstable power. Somewhere above, the sky pulsed and unpulsed, the laws of physics themselves bending under the will of the being before him—the owner of BEAST, the master of realities, a creature who could fold universes and flip them as easily as turning pages in a book.
The figure emerged from the center of the storm, impossibly tall, draped in fabrics that refracted light and shadow across multiple dimensions simultaneously. Its eyes glowed with endless depth, radiating the authority of an entity that had long since transcended conventional power. Sybrian could feel the energy pressing on his mind, reshaping perception, twisting gravity, and warping time. Every step the being took warped the terrain—mountains melted into molten rivers, rivers solidified into jagged stone, and the very air twisted like smoke caught in a violent gale.
“Ah, Sybrian Akaku,” the entity said, voice layered across countless echoes, as if spoken in every language across all dimensions simultaneously. “I have watched your rise. Your rifts, your Bladehounds, your Sus Monkeys… all of it is child’s play. I am the author of reality. And today, your story ends.”
Sybrian’s hands tightened around the Rift of Infinite Allies. He activated the swirling vortex beneath him, attempting to pull himself, Bladehound, Pluppy, and the Sus Monkeys into a safe dimension. The vortex shimmered, a bright spiral of energy designed to transport them away from Pyroclast-like chaos—or anything else that threatened them.
Then, in an instant, BEAST moved—or rather, reality itself obeyed his will. In less than a second, the rift was gone. It vanished as though erased, flipped aside like a page in a massive cosmic book. Sybrian felt the violent displacement in his very body, a pull that nearly ripped him apart. Bladehound yelped as his Fighting Form was destabilized mid-air. Pluppy’s chaotic energy scattered into unstable micro-rifts. The Sus Monkeys clung desperately to fragments of the vortex, chattering in panic as their sanctuary dissolved.
“You cannot run. You cannot hide,” BEAST said, every word shaking the warped plains. “Every rift, every trick, every ally you summon—I control it all. Your powers are ink on my page, Sybrian.”
Sybrian landed hard, energy shields sparking against the warped terrain. He tried again to open another rift, another escape—but again, reality itself rejected it. Each rift flickered, then vanished in an instant, leaving him exposed to the raw, omnipotent force of the villain. Lava and molten rock exploded beneath his feet, and lightning arcs fractured unpredictably across the battlefield. Every movement he made was anticipated, every strike countered before it fully existed.
BEAST advanced, and with each step, the battlefield reshaped violently. Lava became ice, mountains dissolved, gravity shifted, and the air itself resisted Sybrian’s energy. A strike of raw elemental force from BEAST hurled Sybrian across fractured plains, sending him tumbling over jagged terrain. He struggled to stabilize, but even his God-style anticipation could only give him fractions of a second to react. The Rift of Infinite Allies was useless; Bladehound could not maintain Fighting Form; Pluppy’s energy flared chaotically, and the Sus Monkeys scattered. Sybrian had never faced a being capable of manipulating reality at this level—his mind strained against the unrelenting pressure, the world bending violently with every heartbeat.
Desperation clawed at him. He summoned Bladehound into Fighting Form anyway, leaping into the chaotic air to try a flanking maneuver, only for BEAST to twist the landscape again. Platforms evaporated beneath him; lava jets erupted where none existed moments before. Bladehound was destabilized, and Sybrian felt his body slam into molten rock that had appeared instantly. Sparks and flames leapt over him, a storm of destruction that seemed impossible to survive.
Every instinct, every premonition, every trick he had ever used in the Rift or God style was rendered useless. For the first time, Sybrian realized the full extent of BEAST’s power. Every attempt to manipulate the battlefield, every energy strike, every ally summoned into combat—flipped, erased, or repelled in under a second. The villain could rewrite existence as casually as flipping a page, and nothing Sybrian knew could match it.
It was then that the heir—the brother of Vanguard’s fallen royal sibling—arrived. The younger warrior had trained under Sybrian in the secret God style, mastering anticipation, preemptive strikes, and energy integration. He landed beside Sybrian, energy radiating around him in synchronized arcs, Blade and lightning fusing perfectly. With a single motion, he deflected one of BEAST’s strikes, redirecting the force just enough to give Sybrian space to recover. The Rift of Infinite Allies flickered briefly in his presence, stabilized not by conventional power but by the heir’s mastery of God-style energy, bending the edges of reality around him, however briefly.
“Focus,” the heir said, voice steady despite the chaos. “We survive, we adapt, and we strike. This is no ordinary battle.”
The two moved in tandem. Bladehound stabilized in Fighting Form, Pluppy channeled chaotic energy to disrupt the distortions, and the Sus Monkeys leaped unpredictably across the warped battlefield, their numbers creating moments of distraction and openings. Sybrian coordinated with the heir, launching attacks preemptively, striking where BEAST had already unconsciously calculated that no one could reach. Together, they forced micro-gaps in reality, exploiting cracks that even BEAST could not instantaneously erase.
Then, finally, after what felt like hours compressed into seconds, they saw their chance. BEAST extended an arm, ready to flip another portion of the battlefield and crush them, but the heir moved with perfect precision, initiating a synchronized strike. Sybrian channeled energy into Bladehound, who launched in perfect timing with Pluppy’s chaotic surge. The combined assault hit BEAST at the exact instant the villain attempted to manipulate reality again. The energy overload disrupted BEAST’s manipulation temporarily, fracturing his hold on the dimension for the first time.
Sybrian’s hands glowed, the Rift of Infinite Allies shimmering violently, the God-style energy in the heir’s movements perfectly integrated. With a final, synchronized strike, the combined force of preemptive God-style anticipation, dimensional energy, Bladehound, Pluppy, and the Sus Monkeys converged on BEAST. The universe seemed to hold its breath as the cosmic villain staggered, the immense power of his reality manipulation flickering.
Though BEAST survived, he had been hit—the first time his mastery had ever faltered. Sybrian and the heir steadied themselves, their eyes locked on the master of reality, aware that this was only the beginning. The battlefield still roiled with instability, but for the first time, a glimmer of hope pierced the chaos: even BEAST could be challenged, even flipped pages could be seized.
The air vibrated with tension, molten rivers paused mid-flow, lightning crackled like frozen threads, and the battlefield waited, as if acknowledging that the war for reality itself had entered a new, dangerous phase—one where Sybrian and his allies might survive, adapt, and eventually turn the tide.

Epilogue: The Silent Blade
The battlefield was a quiet ruin now, a shattered landscape scarred by the titanic forces that had collided moments ago. Lava had cooled into jagged black stone, rivers of molten energy had solidified into sharp glassy ridges, and the air itself hung heavy with the lingering scent of ozone, smoke, and charred ether. Sybrian stood in the distance, surveying the devastation, exhaustion etched across his face. His body still pulsed with the strain of rifts, Bladehound energy, and the constant battle against a force that had reshaped reality itself. Every fiber of his being screamed in fatigue, yet his eyes burned with resolve. He had faced BEAST, survived, and held the battlefield—barely.
But he was not alone in this silent aftermath. From the edge of the ruins, where shadows pooled and the remnants of war twisted unnaturally, a figure emerged. CFU—Cyborg Fighter Ultimate—stepped lightly across the fractured terrain, his movements precise, fluid, and eerily quiet. The high-tech augmentation covering his face reflected the dying light of the fractured sky, lenses glowing faintly as sensors analyzed the aftermath. His robotic back whirred softly with servo adjustments, and his presence carried an authority that made even the scattered Sus Monkeys fall silent in cautious observation.
BEAST walked away from the wreckage, massive and imposing even in the absence of battle, reality itself seeming to bow subtly to his steps. The air vibrated around him, a faint distortion signaling the remnants of his power still tethered to the fractured world. His aura radiated dominance, a reminder of the immeasurable force he wielded—an entity who could bend, fold, and flip entire universes as easily as flipping a page. Sybrian and the heir, exhausted beyond measure, watched from a distance, unsure if they could intervene again.
CFU, however, did not hesitate. His movements were deliberate, controlled, almost ritualistic. With barely a flick of his wrist, a blade flashed from a sheath on his back, the edges humming with a cold, lethal resonance. Step by step, he closed the distance to BEAST, each movement silent, each calculation precise. Where Sybrian had struggled against the force of reality itself, CFU seemed to move outside its flow, operating in a space governed only by his precision and deadly intent.
BEAST turned briefly, acknowledging the presence of the cyborg with a glance, an almost imperceptible smirk forming across his impossibly calm expression. There was no threat in the gesture, no fear, only the faint hint of curiosity—as if he recognized the skill of this intruder but deemed it a minor annoyance in the grand scale of existence.
And then, without warning, CFU struck.
The movement was almost imperceptible to the eye. In a heartbeat, a flash of steel, a whisper of motion, and the blade had found its mark. BEAST’s head was cleaved in a single, surgical strike, the sound of metal slicing through impossibly dense energy echoing faintly across the ruined plains. He staggered slightly, reality momentarily warping around him, yet CFU did not pause.
The cyborg turned, his motions flawless and calm, retracting the blade with the precision of a machine, his gaze steady as if nothing extraordinary had just occurred. The world seemed to hold its breath. The fires of the battlefield flickered, molten rivers stilled, and even the shards of rift energy that had remained hovered silently in the air. CFU stepped away, each movement deliberate, leaving behind the fallen titan. The ground where BEAST had stood seemed emptier, the massive presence gone, yet no one dared approach.
Sybrian and his allies observed from afar, unable to comprehend fully what had just occurred. Bladehound shifted into Normal Form, Pluppy’s chaotic energy settled into sparks of subdued luminescence, and the Sus Monkeys sat in quiet confusion. Sybrian clenched his fists, the exhaustion of the battle threatening to overwhelm him, but a profound sense of awe filled his chest. CFU had appeared, acted, and disappeared with lethal precision, and whatever force had controlled even Sybrian’s rifts had been met and cut down in a single, fluid strike.
The cyborg disappeared into the shadows, walking away from the battlefield as if he were nothing more than a shadow himself—an agent of judgment who had passed through reality, executed the task, and left no trace beyond the silence and the absence of the impossible.
In the stillness, the battlefield remained quiet. The molten plains cooled, jagged rock hardened, and the air stilled. Sybrian, exhausted and battered, watched the horizon, uncertainty pressing on his mind, yet he dared not speak. CFU’s presence had ensured that the threat of BEAST was, for the moment, gone—but the void left behind, the sheer scale of the power that had been felled, and the manner of its disappearance left a tension in the air that refused to dissipate.
No one approached the fallen BEAST. No one questioned the victory. Only the wind whispered across the blackened plains, carrying the faint echo of the cyborg’s departure. CFU had acted, the impossible had been undone, and he had walked away—silent, precise, and unyielding. And in the minds of all who witnessed it, the conclusion was simple: the owner of BEAST had been defeated.
Yet the shadows seemed to flicker just slightly in the corner of perception, bending the light where they should not bend, as if the universe itself hesitated before fully acknowledging the end. But no one spoke. No one knew. CFU had left, and the battlefield, scarred and silent, was theirs to contemplate—an end, at least for now.
